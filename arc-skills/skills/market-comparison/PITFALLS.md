# Market Comparison Pitfalls

## Rubric-before-neighbors (direction reversal)

**Trap:** Designing rubric axes before studying neighbors. Axes then reflect what you already plan to build → rubric becomes self-justification, not a market test. You get "our thing is fast, so speed is an axis" — and miss what the market actually judges on.

**Guard:** Find 8 neighbors first. Read them. Axes emerge from what neighbors cover; bars set at best-or-average. Rubric is the *output* of neighbor study, not the input.

## Survivorship bias

**Trap:** Only finding successful neighbors. Dead projects, failed launches, and abandoned repos don't show up in "top 10" lists — but they hold the most useful failure data.

**Guard:** Deliberately search for failures. `"X alternatives"` threads often surface why people left. GitHub archived repos with high star counts = failed pivots worth reading.

## Landing-page scoring

**Trap:** Scoring neighbors from their marketing page, not the actual artifact. Every landing page claims "simple" and "powerful" — scoring from copy is scoring fiction.

**Guard:** Score only from the actual thing: read the repo, watch the video, try the product, read the full article. If the artifact is inaccessible, score that axis as N/A and note it.

## Gap misread — nobody wants it solved

**Trap:** Finding an axis where all neighbors score low and declaring "opportunity!" — when the low scores exist because the audience doesn't care about that axis.

**Guard:** For each gap axis, ask: "If someone scored 10/10 here, would the audience switch?" If the answer is unclear, the gap might be rational market behavior, not an oversight.

## False precision

**Trap:** Scoring 7.3 vs 6.8 and treating the difference as meaningful. Human judgment on qualitative axes has ±1.5 noise at best.

**Guard:** Use integers. If two neighbors feel the same, give them the same score. A tie is more honest than a fake decimal.

## Anchor to our solution

**Trap:** After designing to the gap (step 6), re-scoring at publish time with the same rubric but different implicit weights — inflating axes we improved and deflating axes we ignored.

**Guard:** The rubric is frozen after step 3. Same axes, same scoring guide, at publish. If an axis needs to change weight, that's a new comparison, not a gate pass.

## Neighbor count as ritual

**Trap:** Finding exactly 8 neighbors and stopping even when the 9th would change the picture, or forcing 8 when only 4 real competitors exist.

**Guard:** 8 is a forcing function, not a magic number. If the field has 4 real players, say so — a sparse field is signal. If 12 surface, pick the 8 most relevant. The goal is breadth, not a count.

## Recency bias

**Trap:** Overweighting the newest, shiniest neighbor and underweighting the boring incumbent that's been solving the problem for 10 years.

**Guard:** Weight by audience signal (stars, users, revenue), not by age. The boring incumbent might have 100x the users of the new thing.
