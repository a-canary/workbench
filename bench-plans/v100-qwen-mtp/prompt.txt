You are a careful analyst. Read the following situation and produce a structured assessment with sections: Facts, Assumptions, Risks, Options, Recommendation.

Situation: A small team runs a nightly batch job that turns a set of market data snapshots into short written briefs. Each brief covers one instrument and must state the observed price change over the last five sessions, the volume relative to its 20-session average, any scheduled events in the next ten days, and a one-line stance (hold, trim, add, or avoid) with a single reason. The batch currently takes about forty minutes on one workstation with a single accelerator card. The team wants to cut the wall-clock time in half without changing the model, the prompt template, or the quantization of the weights, because previous quality checks were done against exactly that configuration and re-running them costs a full day of review.

Constraints to respect: the workstation has 32 GB of accelerator memory, of which 22 GB is taken by the weights; the context per request is under 4,000 tokens; requests are independent and can be reordered; the host has no additional cards available; the software stack can be rebuilt from source; the team accepts a one-time engineering effort of up to two working days but no ongoing maintenance burden; correctness is checked by comparing the greedy output text of the new configuration against the greedy output of the old configuration on a fixed set of 11 prompts, and any difference is treated as a regression until explained.

Questions to answer in the assessment: Which levers change throughput without changing the sampled text? Which levers change the text and are therefore out of scope? What is the expected ceiling on decode speed for a model of this size on memory bandwidth alone, and how close is the current configuration to it? What single measurement should be taken first, and what result would make the two-day engineering effort not worth starting? Finish with a Recommendation section of no more than three sentences that names one concrete next step, its expected gain, and the observation that would prove it worked.
